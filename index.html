TL;DR

Imagine the Future. Predict Its Motion.

WorldFlow jointly generates future video and explicit 3D motion, combining large-scale visual pretraining with geometric supervision for more generalizable motion prediction.

Abstract

Predicting future physical states stands as a central challenge in embodied intelligence. Existing video world models leave 3D dynamics implicit, action-based formulations remain tied to specific robot configurations, and 3D motion prediction models use flow-only supervision, providing a limited learning signal for physical dynamics. To circumvent these limitations, we introduce WorldFlow, a generative world model that jointly predicts future video and query-based 3D trajectories. By parameterizing 3D motion as continuous displacement tokens aligned with the video grid, a unified Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) backbone jointly denoises both modalities under a rectified-flow objective. This formulation couples explicit geometric dynamics with visual evolution, effectively unlocking the physical commonsense embedded within pretrained video models. To empirically scale and validate this unified architecture, we train WorldFlow on a heterogeneous mixture of 1.44 million object-level samples spanning simulated and real-world domains. We further introduce WorldFlowBench, comprising 1,678 evaluation samples from 11 data sources across simulated and real-world interactions, including spatial reasoning tasks. Across all three evaluation sets, WorldFlow achieves the lowest average displacement error (ADE) and final displacement error (FDE) among the evaluated learning-based methods. Notably, on the challenging SpatialCog set, WorldFlow achieves an ADE of 0.060 m, a 54.2% reduction compared with the strongest external baseline. It also reduces ADE and FDE by 55.9% and 68.5%, respectively, compared with its flow-only variant.

Why Joint Video-Flow Prediction?

Geometry alone does not constitute a full world model. While 3D trajectories explicitly track positions over time, they compress away the core physical constraints—such as object boundaries, surface textures, and environmental affordances—that define real-world interaction. Consequently, a motion model trained strictly on sparse 3D coordinates inevitably suffers from generalization collapse when encountering novel scenes or tasks requiring intricate spatial reasoning.

Joint video-flow prediction bridges this cognitive gap. Pixels provide the dense, scalable signal necessary to unlock the rich physical and visual priors accumulated through large-scale video pretraining. Instead of treating motion as isolated geometric vectors, WorldFlow forces the model to synthesize the scene's appearance and its underlying 3D trajectories simultaneously, embedding explicit motion within a global physical and semantic context.

Core Argument 1: Joint generation outperforms flow-only training

The flow-only variant shares WorldFlow’s DiT Transformer backbone, training data, and optimization settings. The variant predicts only 3D flow, whereas the full model jointly generates video and flow. Joint generation reduces ADE by 48.5%, 19.8%, and 55.9% on Sim7, Real3, and SpatialCog, respectively, and lowers FDE on all three subsets—including a 68.5% FDE reduction on SpatialCog (0.248 → 0.078 m)—supporting the value of complementary visual supervision for accurate 3D motion.

Core Argument 2: Direct flow generation outperforms post-hoc tracking

Using the same WorldFlow checkpoint and conditioning inputs, directly generated trajectories achieve lower ADE and FDE and higher PWT than Track4World and 4RC applied to its generated videos on all three subsets. This supports explicit 3D flow as a generative output rather than recovering motion through a separate tracking stage.

Future Work

We aim to scale both the volume and diversity of flow data, with particular emphasis on reliable real-world annotations and broader coverage of motion patterns, interactions, and camera configurations. We also plan to extend joint generation to include robot actions alongside video and 3D flow. By progressively establishing reliable performance and generalization for each modality, we seek to understand whether jointly modeling video, flow, and action yields synergistic benefits in predicting future world evolution and guiding manipulation.
